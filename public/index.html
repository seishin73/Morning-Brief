How stories are chosen. Every candidate is scored on five signals — how many independent outlets carry it, how recently it broke, the standing of the outlet, how well it fits the topic, and how high it sat on its own section front. Corroboration is weighted heaviest, so the tally marks beside each headline are close to the ranking itself. Near-duplicates are clustered across the whole pool before any topic is considered, so a story carried by one outlet's world desk and another's politics desk still counts as two.
Sourcing. Headlines link to the original publication. Summaries come from each outlet's own feed, cleaned up; no article text is reproduced. Photographs come from the publisher's feed where there is one, then the article's own social image, then a freely-licensed photograph of the subject from Wikimedia Commons.
This build. Live. Feeds are read directly from around seventy newsrooms and re-read every twenty minutes; ranking, clustering and assembly run on your own machine.
